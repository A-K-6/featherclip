FeatherClip 2.0:
a native home for your clipboard.

A clipboard app should feel like a small part of the desktop you already know.

By Aeen Karimian · 3 minute read

The idea behind FeatherClip is simple: sometimes the thing you need is the thing you copied three copies ago. A small history popup gives you a way back. Open it, choose a clip, paste it into the app you were using, and carry on.

Version 2.0 brings that idea to native interfaces on both macOS and Ubuntu. The Tauri interface has been replaced by Swift and AppKit on Mac, and Rust with GTK4 and libadwaita on Ubuntu.

Glass that belongs to the Mac

On macOS 26 and newer, FeatherClip uses Apple’s own glass view. Search, menus, buttons, and the clipboard list are native AppKit controls. On macOS 13–15, the popup uses the system’s translucent material.

The popup stays a separate floating panel. It can appear over full-screen Spaces, and it doesn’t become a tab inside another window. Press ⌘⇧V to bring it up.

A familiar place on Ubuntu

The Ubuntu app uses GTK4 and libadwaita, following GNOME’s light and dark appearance. There’s a small list, search, Pause, Clear, and a menu for the few other options.

GNOME Wayland has its own clipboard and focus rules. The installer includes a small GNOME Shell bridge so FeatherClip can read copies and paste back into the previous window. A first install may need a logout and login before GNOME discovers it.

Keep the history small

FeatherClip remembers up to 30 unique clips, with 24 MiB available for retained clipboard payloads. History stays in memory and disappears when you quit. It begins with new copies after launch; paused copies aren’t backfilled when you resume.

Text, PNG/TIFF images, and local file references are supported. File contents aren’t stored: the original files need to remain available. Rich text becomes plain text, and app-specific formats aren’t retained.

Select a clip. Return to your work.

Keep your destination app active before opening FeatherClip. Select a clip to copy it, restore that app, and send Paste. Shift+Enter copies without pasting.

On Mac, sending Paste requires FeatherClip’s Accessibility permission. A replaced, ad-hoc signed build can leave an old permission entry behind, even if its switch looks enabled. If a popup closes without pasting, the setup guide walks through refreshing that entry.

Try it, or make it yours

There are separate installers for Apple Silicon Macs, Intel Macs, and Ubuntu 24.04+ amd64. The project is MIT licensed, and its source and build checks are public.
